The MVP path

Photo in. Spoken correction out.
Nothing leaves the phone.

Five stages, all local. Three of them are AI, and two of them exist specifically so that the AI is never the thing deciding whether a child's maths is right.

The tutor pipeline camera → ink → parse → solve → speak
  1. 01 · Capture

    CameraX. Page detection and a single still frame of the working. No video stream and no continuous inference — one shot, because the phone has to survive thirty hours of this.

  2. 02 · Read the ink

    ML Kit Digital Ink Recognition for stroke input, Text Recognition v2 for photographed pages. Lines are segmented into individual steps, each with a confidence score. Anything under 90% halts and asks the student to confirm — the machine never judges work it isn't sure it read.

  3. 03 · Parse

    Each confirmed line becomes a symbolic expression. This is ordinary parsing, not inference, and it either succeeds or it fails loudly.

  4. 04 · Check equivalence

    The solver asks one question per line: is step n still algebraically equal to step n−1? The first line where that stops being true is the error. One code path covers transposition, arithmetic, factorisation and calculus, and it is deterministic — the language model is not consulted.

  5. 05 · Explain

    Gemma-2-2B, quantised, on-device. The model receives the solver's trace and writes one short sentence per step. It phrases; it does not adjudicate. Android TTS then speaks it in Tamil, Hindi or English from a voice pack that lives on the device.

CameraX  ·  ML Kit  ·  symbolic solver  ·  Gemma-2-2B via MediaPipe LLM Inference  ·  Android TTS

The model never decides whether the maths is right. A solver does.

That split is the whole safety argument. A wrong correction on a child's homework isn't a bad output, it's a harm — so the part that can hallucinate is only ever allowed to write the sentence, never the verdict.

Who this is for

A class of sixty, and one teacher.

In overcrowded rural and government classrooms nobody gets one-to-one feedback. A student with a learning gap — or early dyscalculia — copies the final answer off the board and never finds out which of their own six lines was the one that broke. The gap compounds for years, and it compounds silently.

The specific failure

Not "got it wrong". A student writes 3x + 5 = 20, then 3x = 25. They added the five instead of subtracting it. Marking the answer wrong teaches them nothing. Pointing at line two teaches them the rule.

Why it has to be offline

These are the classrooms with one bar of signal and a data pack that ran out on the twentieth. A tutor that needs a round trip is a tutor that spins. Every feature here completes with the radios switched off.

Why it has to speak

A child who is behind in maths is often also behind in English. The correction is spoken in Tamil or Hindi, because an explanation in a second language is not an explanation.

The connectivity contract

Bad internet is the country.
Not no internet.

One bar, 2G fallback, packet loss, a hostel basement, a village at six in the evening. Apps don't fail cleanly there — they hang, retry and spin. So connectivity is treated as a dial, not a switch, and the app is built to be best at the bottom of it.

T0 — Dark no network at all

Every feature completes, fully. The scan, the solver, the explanation and the voice are all local, so there is nothing to degrade. This is the design target, and it is what the demo opens on with airplane mode visibly switched on.

the design target

T1 — Weak 2G · one bar

Progress records and class deltas sync opportunistically; media queues. Everything is compressed and resumable, and the UI never blocks on any of it. The student never learns that the network is bad.

faster, not possible

T2 — Good real bandwidth, briefly

Burst sync. A genuinely hard explanation may escalate to cloud — but only when the local verifier reports low confidence and the network is actually good. Local-first, cloud-optional, which is also how the free AI credits get used honestly.

richer, still optional

The design rule: every feature must complete at T0. T1 and T2 make it faster or richer — never possible. Flip the strip on the phone above and check that claim yourself.

The technical heart

EduQoo Core — a local agent runtime.

Anyone can call a local model. The harder problem is making a 2B model reliable enough to be trusted with a child's homework, on a phone, with no network to fall back on. That harness is the project; the tutor is what it is pointed at.

EduQoo Core one process, on the phone
  1. Router

    Local-first. Escalates to cloud only when the verifier reports low confidence and the network is genuinely good.

  2. Context budgeter

    Token accounting against a finite attention budget, with remaining capacity fed back after every tool call.

  3. Assembler

    system │ core memory │ retrieved context │ scratchpad

  4. Planner loop

    Step limit, timeout, cancel. It is allowed to give up rather than grind the battery.

  5. Tool registry

    Typed Kotlin schema compiled to JSON Schema. Seven tools, deliberately non-overlapping — near-duplicates make a small model burn turns choosing.

    • ink.read()ML Kit — photo or strokes → lines + confidence
    • solve.check()is step n still equivalent to step n−1?
    • solve.steps()problem → a correct, ordered solution trace
    • speak()Android TTS, regional language, offline voice pack
    • retrieve()SQLite vector store, 768-dim, plus an FTS table
    • class.broadcast()push to every joined device over SSE
    • progress.write()per-student concept record for the dashboard
  6. Two-pass executor

    Pass 1 unconstrained — the model decides which tools to call, and they run. Pass 2 schema-constrained — the results are already in context and the model only formats the answer.

  7. Verifier

    Symbolic ground truth for anything mathematical, a grounding check against the retrieved source for anything factual, schema validation for structure. Nothing reaches a student unchecked.

  8. Memory

    core (pinned, ~120 tokens) │ working (compacted on fill) │ archival

Hard part 01

Rule-based validation, not a careful prompt

Every claim about the mathematics is produced by a deterministic solver and checked before rendering. The model sees the trace and writes prose from it. There is no prompt anywhere in this system asking a language model to mark a child's work, because no prompt makes that safe.

Hard part 02

Confidence gating on the read, not just the answer

Handwritten fractions and superscripts are where recognition breaks. Every line carries a confidence, and anything under threshold halts the pipeline and asks the student to confirm. The confirm step isn't an apology for OCR — it is correct UX: the student verifies the machine understood them before the machine judges them.

Hard part 03

A finite attention budget

Context length degrades quality through lost-in-the-middle effects long before the hard token limit. So: ~120 tokens of pinned core memory, working memory compacted on fill, raw tool results cleared once consumed, a scratchpad written to disk outside the window, and seven deliberately non-overlapping tools.

Why it has to be this device

The phone isn't the screen here.
It's the compute.

The rules ask for on-device inference targeting the Snapdragon NPU, with camera, voice and on-device AI in the build. This design doesn't merely satisfy that — it stops working without it.

On-device inference on the NPU

A quantised Gemma-2-2B loaded through MediaPipe LLM Inference and kept warm, so the first explanation isn't the slow one. The weights ship inside the APK. Nothing is fetched, and there is no key to leak, because there is no endpoint.

Camera, as the primary input

CameraX plus ML Kit is the whole front door. This isn't a chatbot with a camera bolted on — a student who can't type 25/3 can still photograph the page they already wrote.

Voice, as the actual output

Android TextToSpeech called directly, with the regional voice packs downloaded once and then working with the radios off. A written correction in English helps the students who need help least.

Radios and services

The embedded server, the local-only hotspot and a sustained inference loop need real foreground-service lifecycle and real permission handling. Native Kotlin, because accelerator delegates live at the native layer or nowhere.

Office Kit, as a product feature

Not a checkbox. The teacher's diagnostic CSV is pulled to the laptop over the clipboard-and-file bridge while the phone stays on its own hotspot — the analytics story and the Office Kit score in one action.

Thermal honesty

Single-shot inference only. No live camera loop, no back-to-back generation. A phone that has been building for twenty-eight hours throttles, and a demo that ignores that is a demo that dies on stage.

The honest part

What we deliberately do not build.

A clean "no, and here's why" beats a broken yes. These are roadmap, and saying so out loud is how you show you know where the edge is.

Tamil handwriting recognition
ML Kit has no Tamil script model. Tamil is spoken output only, and we say that rather than promising it. The maths itself is digits and Latin, which is well covered.
Auto-grading long-form answers
Marking a paragraph of written reasoning is unreliable, and a wrong grade on a child's work is a real harm — not a rounding error. We localise errors in symbolic working, nothing else.
Live continuous camera explanation
Thermal throttling on a phone that has been building for twenty-eight hours. Single-shot capture only.
Generative video
Not feasible on-device, and it would hallucinate maths. The walkthrough is rendered from the solver trace instead, which is why it cannot show a wrong step.
Cloud accounts, login, multi-school sync
Contradicts the premise. There is no account because there is no server that outlives the class.
A clinical dyscalculia diagnosis
We surface repeated error patterns to a teacher. We do not diagnose anything, and the interface never uses that word about a specific child.
